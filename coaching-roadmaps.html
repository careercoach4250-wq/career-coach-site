What a roadmap looks like

Every roadmap starts from where you are and where you want to end up. Here's a real one — built by career-navigator for a Tulane sophomore, using Tulane's own career-center resources and recruiting timelines, not generic advice.

Green Bull track
1FoundationSophomore Fall›

Get into the two structures that matter most, and stop looking like a freshman on paper.

  • Apply to Green Bull Investment Banking Group the moment applications open — ~20 students/year, the single highest-leverage move available at Tulane for this goal.
  • Join Wall Street Krewe as a lower-bar finance community while the Green Bull decision is pending.
  • Book a first 1:1 with Freeman Career Management Center via Handshake — start the coaching relationship now, not during crunch season.
  • Rebuild the resume in Freeman's recommended format; get it reviewed at a CMC drop-in.
  • Start technical self-study (accounting refresh, DCF basics), ~3 hrs/week.
  • Clean up LinkedIn; follow 5 Tulane alumni in IB via the alumni tool — no ask yet, just visibility.
Milestone by December: Green Bull application submitted, Wall Street Krewe member, first CMC session done, resume rebuilt.
2Break InSophomore Spring›

Land a bank sophomore program — the actual unlock for junior-year recruiting.

  • Apply to bank sophomore/early-ID programs as soon as they open — treat these as the real cycle, not a formality.
  • Freeman's own timeline flags that junior-internship applications can open as early as January — start applying to some this spring too, don't wait for "junior year."
  • Move from self-study into full LBO/DCF practice builds, timed like a real interview.
  • Start deliberate coffee chats: one Tulane alum or 2nd-year analyst per week.
  • Attend every on-campus bank info session, even for firms that aren't the top choice — reps talk to each other, and the practice matters.
Milestone by May: 12+ coffee chats logged, 3+ sophomore programs applied to, one full model built independently.
3The Real RecruitSoph. Summer → Junior Fall›

Convert two years of groundwork into the junior-summer analyst offer.

  • Sophomore summer: complete whichever program was secured — or use the summer for a smaller local finance internship or continued technical drilling.
  • Junior fall is peak season: expect first-round interviews to run through the semester. Keep two years of coffee-chat relationships warm rather than cold-starting.
  • Step into a leadership role inside Green Bull or Wall Street Krewe — a credibility signal that also compounds the network.
  • Keep the consulting backup warm at near-zero cost: Tulane's own timeline shows consulting's junior-internship applications open later, so this doesn't compete with the IB push.
Milestone by December of junior year: summer analyst offer in hand — or a clear fallback activated via the later consulting cycle.
4Prove ItJunior Summer›

Turn the internship into a return offer.

  • Treat the ten weeks as a ten-week interview: over-deliver, ask for feedback proactively, build real relationships beyond the assigned buddy.
  • Stay lightly in touch with Freeman CMC and Green Bull mentors through the summer.
  • Decide now whether CFA Level I is worth starting before senior year gets busy.
Milestone: return offer extended before the internship ends (or an immediate pivot into full-time recruiting).
5Close It OutSenior Year›

Finish strong — don't let recruiting momentum eclipse graduation requirements.

  • If the return offer is accepted: stay engaged with the team all year.
  • If not: run full-time recruiting in parallel with coursework, leaning on the same network.
  • Confirm Tulane's public-service graduation requirement is fully satisfied.
Milestone: signed offer, start date confirmed, graduation requirements complete.
6LaunchYear 1 on the Job›

Transition well, and stay a node in the Tulane network rather than disappearing from it.

  • Join the Tulane NYC alumni club within the first few months in the city.
  • Consider becoming a Green Bull mentor the following year.
  • Revisit CFA Level I timing once the role clarifies the long-term direction.
Milestone: fully onboarded, one Tulane-network touchpoint maintained per quarter.

The reference toolkit

Skills to develop, in order

  1. Financial modeling — 3-statement, DCF, LBO, comps
  2. Accounting fundamentals, building on coursework already in progress
  3. Excel fluency — shortcuts, speed, banker formatting conventions
  4. Markets literacy — a daily habit, not a cram subject
  5. Behavioral storytelling — a tight "why banking, why this bank"
  6. Coffee-chat conversation skill — practiced, not innate

Clubs, programs & certifications

  • Green Bull IB Group — top priority
  • Wall Street Krewe — baseline community
  • Freeman CMC coaching — ongoing throughout
  • Darwin Fenner Fund — aspirational, junior year
  • CFA Level I — optional, decide by junior summer
  • Tulane Consulting Group — kept warm as backup

Networking playbook

  • LinkedIn's Tulane alumni tool as the starting point for every outreach
  • One coffee chat per week, sustained — not just a pre-recruiting sprint
  • Green Bull's alumni-mentor pairing, if admitted
  • Tulane's active NYC alumni club as a standing touchpoint
  • Every campus info session, even for a non-top-choice firm

Internship & application strategy

  • Apply early, not "on time" — Freeman's own guide flags January applications, earlier than most students expect
  • Run IB and consulting in parallel without diluting focus — their cycles don't actually overlap
  • Prioritize firms where a Green Bull connection already exists
  • Treat every sophomore "insight" program as a real audition, not a formality

Specific next steps, starting this week

  1. This week: book a first Freeman CMC coaching appointment via Handshake.
  2. This week: check Green Bull's application window and start the application.
  3. Within 2 weeks: rebuild the resume in Freeman's format; get it reviewed at a CMC drop-in.
  4. Within 3 weeks: send the first 5 LinkedIn connection requests to Tulane alumni in IB.
  5. Within 4 weeks: start a financial-modeling fundamentals module, 3 hrs/week.
  6. Starting now, ongoing: read the markets section of a major financial paper daily, 15 minutes.
